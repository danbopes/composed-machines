# Changelog

Revision history of the composed-machine proposal (the page, `docs/index.html`, and its companion documents). Revisions not listed here were not recorded.

## Revision 5, 2026-11-03 (adversarial review)

Changes to `docs/WRITEUP.md`, `docs/V2-PARTS.md` and `README.md`:

- The spoil modifier is out of v1 and offered as a separately refusable add-on; the cold box example uses `spoil_modifier = {multiplier = 8, requires_power = true}` on an electric 10 kW source.
- The dig-site example keeps pY's mechanics: electric source, a filtered food input, `consume_at = "script"`, creature module slots, and the no-creature and stop-at-1000 conditions.
- Event delivery is stated as new (queued during the entity update, raised right after it), with synchronous delivery as raise_on_crafted does it as the fallback.
- The list of what today's workarounds cannot do was corrected against features 2.1 already has: the `inventory` GUI element (2.1.7), container rotation (2.1.7), `disabled_by_script`, `LuaEntity.energy` on an energy interface, and player inventory events.
- One coverage statement everywhere (22 of 189 surveyed buildings fully, 36 partly); emulation ratios are stated as times the per-entity tick cost of the native parts, and the cold box figure as a ceiling.
- The writeup was reordered (design, why not an ECS, why not more fields, the problem) and gained a delivery order with a v0 step; the C++ line-count estimate was removed.
- References to working notes that are not in the repository were removed.

## Revision 3 (benchmarks), 2026-11-02

The bench markers were filled from `bench/RESULTS.md` (the quiet-box runs: 3600 ticks, 5 runs, CPUs 8-11). Filled: the header note on markers; in the problem section, the pY family instance counts from the census of the three saves, and the 3600-tick pY timing runs; in the ECS section, the native matrix at N = 2000 and 20000 (chest, idle furnace, electric furnace, stone furnace, assembler), the quiet-box per-class machine columns, mulark's test-000060 per-class figures, and the eight composed-against-native pairs with each one's scriptUpdate share. The busy-box figures and the 30 ns times D over A projection were kept beside them. New from the census: no derrick -base assembler was found in any pY save.

Eight more buildings were then taken straight from the forum and GitHub asks and built as today's workaround (`docs/TEST-ENTITIES-2.md`), so that the proposed prototype, the workaround it replaces and the workaround's cost sit side by side: a hearth that burns whether or not it works (t=61277, t=108882), a brazier that announces when its fuel runs out (t=4841, t=64769), a creep collector that spends energy per unit of script-chosen work (Rampant #64), a furnace whose speed is a signal-bound setting (t=113922, t=113731), a mass driver that charges and fires (Renai's cannon, t=124752), a slag generator (t=120072), a smelter that heats a pipe network while it works (t=130444), and a 6x3 storehouse shell with no Lua at all, kept as a measurement of what a recipe-less shell costs by itself. Where a mod ships the building the old way, that workaround is a third benchmark column: Rampant's energy interface polled every 60 ticks, Renai's every-tick dispatcher, the hidden beacon per machine that local_effect retired in 2.1.13. What the emulations cannot do is the same list as before, and it is the list the native type is for: no event when fuel enters a reactor or a signal changes, so a cold hearth and a wired furnace poll forever (20 and 30 ticks); detection latency of one poll on every transition; an assembler takes its ingredient at the start of the cycle, so consume_at = "finish" is unreachable; a recipe cannot carry an energy or a heat product, so both ride in a Lua add per finished craft; a 6x3 shell cannot turn a quarter in the engine and a script write to direction is ignored, so the storehouse flips 180 degrees by replacing itself, losing its unit number (corrected in revision 5: containers rotate since 2.1.7 with `direction_count`, and the remaining cost is a script handler on every rotate, build and blueprint path); a hidden heat interface keeps its prototype's connection side when the core rotates; an empty-recipe shell starts its next craft on the tick the last one ends, before Lua can disable it. Each is a named KNOWN-LIMIT test in the suite, not a silent skip.

Measured (`bench/RESULTS.md` section b; N = 2000, 3600 ticks, 3 runs, CPUs 8-11; ns per instance per tick, emulation against native equivalent): hearth 234 against 101 for a stone furnace with a bare reactor (2.3x; the 20-tick temperature poll is 125 ns of it); brazier 111 against 53 (2.1x; the 30-tick fuel poll is 61 ns); creep collector 390 against 49 for an electric furnace (7.9x), and against 194 for the 60-tick energy-interface poll Rampant would use (2.0x); storehouse 114 against 94 for two steel chests (1.2x), with no Lua at all; tuned furnace 143 against 74 with local_effect written once (1.9x) and 98 for the hidden beacon it retired (1.5x); mass driver 40 against 45 for the 15-tick dispatcher (0.9x) and 98 for a gear assembler that never pauses (0.4x); slag generator 125 against 158 for a steam engine plus a stone furnace (0.8x); waste-heat smelter 122 against 113 for an electric furnace plus a resting heat interface (1.1x). Each ratio is times the per-entity tick cost of the native parts. Two readings came out of these runs that the earlier ones had missed. A handler on the crafted event runs inside the entity update, so its Lua is billed to the AssemblingMachine column and not to scriptUpdate: the creep collector's harvest is 355 ns per instance there while scriptUpdate reads 7, and every 120 ticks all 2000 shells finish together in a 78 ms tick. That is the likely source of the unexplained assembler-column gap on the dig site and the seep derrick; the two columns have to be read together. And a recipe-less assembler shell never leaves the update list: the storehouse costs 18 ns per instance with nothing to do, the same as 2000 working gear assemblers, while its 28,000 proxy doors cost nothing measurable. Ratios under 1 say the native equivalent does more work, not that the emulation is cheap. The suite was 299 tests, 288 passing, 11 failing on purpose. A second adversarial review (`mod/component-lab/REVIEW-2.md`) found two serious faults in the later entities, a storehouse turn that ignored water and ghosts and a fuel door an inserter could drain; both are fixed with regression tests.
