# Composed machines: a writeup for Wube

*How a load-time-composed modding entity would work.*

> Public copy. Engine-internal references from the private working notes have been removed; where a claim rested on them it is marked as an assumption for Wube to confirm.

Revision 3, 2026-11-02. Companions: `docs/SPEC.md` (the field-level spec the reference mod mirrors) and `docs/ENTITIES.md` with `docs/TEST-ENTITIES-2.md` (what the reference mod builds and measures). Benchmark numbers marked quiet-box come from `bench/RESULTS.md` (one pass, 3600 ticks, 5 runs, CPUs 8-11 pinned); every marker is filled. Every other number carries its source and its noise.

On 2018-06-23 Rseding91 wrote, about a general-purpose modding entity: "I'm not against that concept but I've yet to see anyone write up how such an entity would work ... If someone did that it would go a long way towards me thinking about adding it to the game." The next day: "see if it can actually work to make such an entity where parts of it can just be shut off" (forums.factorio.com, p=367885, read in full). This is that writeup: one new static entity type, composed-machine, whose optional parts are chosen when the prototype loads, which joins the update list only when a part has to tick, and which tells Lua about edges through opt-in events. Section 3 says why it is not the entity component system he rejected in the same thread.

## 1. The problem

The research sweep (`research/RESEARCH.md`: forum requests, help threads, the local mod library, the nine pY mods) counted thirteen capability gaps by how many distinct threads and mods show them. The top ten, with hits: compound-entity lifecycle glue (20); polling because the event is missing (18); a script-writable circuit output on a type that lacks one (13); a machine shell with no recipe (12); an energy source on a type without one (12); more or differently shaped inventories (11); runtime-settable effects (10); a fluid box on a type without one (8); an inventory spoil-rate modifier (8); status honesty without polling (8). The composed machine closes those ten, about 120 of the roughly 134 counted hits, and not script-scaled generation, heat output or moving units (gaps 11 to 13, 14 hits).

In a save those gaps are 41 shipped compound entities (`RESEARCH.md` section 2; `SPEC.md` appendix A: 8 collapse fully, 9 partly). pY's dino dig site is an assembling machine with a fixed recipe, permanently disabled by script, plus a hidden food container, plus a unit and a target entity per trip, driven every 61 ticks. Its seep derricks are mining drills with a hidden assembler each and a new render object per run, every 67 ticks. corrundum's ice box rewrites every stack's spoil percentage in every box every 53 ticks. Five mods hide a constant combinator behind a type with no script output. 0-things is 23 thousand lines of compound lifecycle glue.

What modders pay, in their own reports: every build path must be handled or parts go missing; blueprints leak hidden parts; cut and copy go through a different event than the blueprint tool; undo needs manual LuaUndoRedoStack edits, and the modder who has "done composite entities every way you can imagine" reports it "mostly" works (p=655527); identity is lost across undo (p=584507); staggered loops make behaviour coarse and the UI dishonest. Wube's verdict on the glue is the same: "At their core compound entities don't work" (Rseding91, 2020-09-13, p=512078).

In a tick: one busy-box run of the pY 134 save (74 mods; `bench/RESULTS.notes.md`) measured 19.95 ms per update, of which scriptUpdate 2.20 ms and Lua GC 1.55 ms, against entityUpdate 6.53 ms (AssemblingMachine 2.46). The disabled-by-script shells cost about nothing in that column; their cost is in the script columns and the extra entities. How many there are, from a census mod run for one benchmark tick (`bench/RESULTS.md` section d; the saves were not modified), in pY 134, pY Season 3 A2 Fixed and pY Fixed: dino dig sites 14, 2 and 14, each with its hidden food container and no digosaur unit out at save time; seep derricks 29, 11 and 29; caravan units 8, 13 and 8 with 1, 26 and 1 outposts; vat brains 0, 1178 and 0, each with a hidden beacon; farm buildings 5100, 1149 and 5056; TURD hidden beacons 2176, 255 and 2176; out of 2.95, 1.36 and 2.94 million entities in all. The census matched no derrick -base assembler prototype in any of the three saves, so the hidden assembler per derrick described above is not confirmed for these mod versions. The quiet-box timing of the three saves (3600 ticks, three runs, pinned to four cores, best run, spread 1.3 to 3.8 percent) puts the script share next to those counts: pY 134 takes 6.12 ms per update, pY Fixed 6.26 and A2 Fixed 2.22. Of each tick, entityUpdate is 1.81, 1.69 and 0.60 ms (27 to 30 percent), and Lua, counted as scriptUpdate plus incremental garbage collection, is 1.75, 1.95 and 0.92 ms: 29, 31 and 41 percent, the same size as the whole entity update, with the collector alone at 0.63 to 0.83 ms. That share belongs to every mod in the save, not only to compound-entity emulation, and the per-mod split was not measured; what the numbers show is that in a real pY base the Lua side is already as large as the entity side, and that every family moved into the engine comes out of that share (`bench/RESULTS.md` section d).

The mods' own GitHub trackers say the same thing in the maintainers' words (`research/github-issues.md`: five search angles, every candidate re-opened and its quote matched before it counted). 176 findings in 100 repositories survived verification, 78 of them matched to portal mods with 5.8 million downloads between them, three quarters of the dated ones from 2025 and 2026. Thirty are a building or mechanic the author abandoned and said why: a dig-site food slot that accepts several foods ("it seems to be an API limitation ... Not going to fix this"), a rotatable rectangular chest (Warehousing and WideChests ship one prototype per orientation), a creep collector that should spend energy per tile ("Factorio API doesn't allow to manage energy consumption directly"), composite power buildings marked immovable because hidden children are not bound to their parent. Seventy-eight describe a hidden-entity workaround, 32 a polling loop, 13 a prototype per value. Thirty-five of the 176 are fixed by v1 as specified and 60 more by a v2 part; 65 are other cores and 11 cross-surface links, which this type does not claim. Of the 87 candidates rejected, 49 were rejected because 2.0 or 2.1 had since shipped the missing piece, which is the pattern this writeup counts on. Two findings filed from the author's own account are flagged in the source file and not cited.

## 2. The design

**Prototype.** type = "composed-machine", inheriting EntityWithOwnerPrototype. Parts, each optional: up to eight named inventories with a role (input, output, storage, trash) that fixes inserter access, filters, a bar and an optional spoil-rate modifier; one energy source from the full union, loaded through the same `energy_source` definition the existing types that take one use; up to eight fluid boxes; a recipe-less process whose ingredients and products are the ordinary recipe structs, started automatically or by script; module slots with an effect receiver; a circuit connector with contents or script-written logistic-section output; up to four engine-evaluated conditions, each declaring its status; up to four blueprintable scalar settings; an activity rule; a tick_interval; opt-in event flags. `SPEC.md` section 1 has the field table and the fifteen validation rules.

**Shapes.** The loader reduces the part set to one of four. S0 passive: no process, nothing to watch per tick. S1 watcher: no process, but a burner that must burn (a burner is assumed to refill from its fuel only during its owner's update; for Wube to confirm), a power-dependent condition or spoil modifier, or a fluid threshold. S2 timer: a process without energy. S3 machine: a process with energy. Each shape is a separate final class; absent parts are empty bases, not null pointers checked per tick. S0 is not updatable, like a container (`LuaEntity.is_updatable` is false for one, and 20000 chests run at the empty-world floor, `bench/RESULTS.md`), so the instance is never in any active list. S1 to S3 are three new entries in the engine's per-type update order (updatability is a side interface with per-type batched updates, Rseding91, 2016, p=154019); an instance with tick_interval above 1 would instead be updated every N ticks, as the engine already does for entities that do not update every tick (beacons, Rseding91, 2024, t=113632). How that scheduling works internally is assumed, for Wube to confirm.

**Updatable iff.** A prototype is updatable iff its shape is not S0. An instance is active iff it is updatable, not deactivated (script, deconstruction, frozen, control behaviour) and not asleep. It sleeps as a crafting machine does, with an O(1) unlink from the intrusive active list (Rseding91, 2016, p=154019, and on Reddit in 2023), which staff have said is safe during the update loop (FFF-204 discussion, 2017, p=303569), on exactly the results only an external action can change (a condition failed, ingredients short, output full, waiting for script, burner empty, activity inventories empty). It is registered on the same wake-up lists inserters already sleep on, and every transfer into or out of its inventories is the wake: an insert wakes what waits on output, a removal what waits on input (assumed from the engine's behaviour; for Wube to confirm). The electric network is not a wake source: an instance waiting on power stays active at zero progress, as a crafter in a brownout is assumed to (for Wube to confirm); and a sleeping electric instance still pays its `drain`, as idle vanilla machines do (20000 idle electric furnaces measure 0.15 ms per update against the chests' 0.11, `bench/RESULTS.md`). tick_interval is the lever, updating every N ticks with an electric buffer the loader sizes for N. Belts and vanilla types stay untouched (Rseding91, 2024-12-23, p=654992).

**Update.** S3's update is the crafting machine's update minus recipe selection: energy source tick (assumed, as for crafters, to be needed only for burner and fluid sources); gates; conditions and start; progress at got over need, the draw dispatching on the source's kind fixed at construction, copied from the crafter's (assumed), so low power slows the cycle; finish through the crafter's product path with productivity as bonus_progress; events queued; status and visuals; sleep decision. S2 drops the energy terms by if constexpr; S1 is the energy tick, the gates, the tick-evaluated conditions and the activity rule. Lua never runs inside any step.

**Events.** Seven, each opt-in per prototype with an id allocated at load like custom-event: cycle_started, cycle_finished, status_changed, inventory_changed, power_state_changed, fluid_threshold, settings_changed. They are queued during the entity phase and dispatched at one flush point, right after the entity update and before the script phase, in update order, coalesced to one per entity per tick; a change after the flush belongs to the next tick. This is new code, plainly: raise_on_crafted runs Lua synchronously inside the crafting update (measured: crafted-event handlers are billed to the AssemblingMachine column, not scriptUpdate, `bench/RESULTS.md` section b; script trigger effects are assumed to behave likewise), and the engine is assumed to have no deferred queue for entity events (for Wube to confirm); this type refuses the synchronous path because a handler could destroy the entity being updated mid-bucket. The flush point sits after the whole entity update, so instances updated every N ticks are covered too (assumed). No per-tick hook of any kind; no inventory copy in any payload (consume_at = "script" hands the handler the live stacks).

**GUI.** Assembled from existing widgets in a fixed order, nothing drawn for an absent part: the crafter frame and status line with the energy source's own widget, as vanilla machines show it; a slot grid per inventory (the 2.1.7 inventory element); the module row; fluid slots; settings; the circuit panel; the tooltip from each part's own description lines, as vanilla tooltips are built. Every action is a normal input action; a relative GUI anchor lets mods attach panels.

**Blueprint, undo, migration.** One entity on the engine's normal paths: blueprint carries bar, filters, circuit state, item requests and settings; undo is one stack item; mining returns every inventory; cloning copies all parts; fast replace transfers inventories by name, and from a plain container into the first storage inventory. The save stores a part mask, then each part's own serialiser; the per-prototype inventory name table goes once into the map's prototype-migration data, so a renamed inventory migrates by name and a changed shape is re-instantiated on load as the class the prototype now implies. A mod leaving a compound entity ships one migrations script: create, move by inventory, destroy the hidden parts.

## 3. Why this is not the ECS that was rejected

Rseding91 rejected runtime component systems in April 2016, June 2018 and three times in November 2018 (the 10th, 12th and 24th), and the model built for this writeup agrees with him. What he left open was one modding entity whose parts can be shut off. Shutting parts off at load, not at runtime, is what the engine already does for energy sources: the `energy_source` field picks one of five kinds (electric, burner, heat, fluid, void) at data load, and each instance keeps that kind for life (public prototype reference). The per-tick cost of that choice in a crafter is assumed to be a dispatch on the fixed kind, with real work only for burner and fluid sources (for Wube to confirm); the composed machine copies the same dispatch. The composed machine extends that from one part to eight, keeps a normal per-entity update, and slots into the existing per-surface type-ordered active list as three more static buckets.

Mechanisms reused, by behaviour: the updatable side interface with O(1) sleep and wake on intrusive active lists (Rseding91, 2016, p=154019; 2023 on Reddit), which containers do not take part in; the per-type batched update over the active list, and the engine's existing less-than-every-tick updates for tick_interval (Rseding91, 2024, t=113632); the crafting machine's sleep and wake and the wake-up lists inserters sleep on; the five energy source kinds and their per-instance creation; the crafter's inventory access and its notification on every transfer; the effect receiver behind module slots and `LuaEntity::local_effect`; logistic sections as the constant combinator uses them; the recipe give, consume and refund paths; the crafter's energy draw; the per-prototype event ids of custom-event and raise_on_crafted; the re-stamp of a stack's spoil tick for the re-key. New code: the shape templating, the condition evaluator, the event queue and flush, the spoil re-stamp hooks on the transfer paths, the energy-draw dispatch (a copy of the crafter's), the GUI composition. `SPEC.md` section 13 lists each fact with its verification state.

**What the model says.** `model/RESULTS.md` is one C++17 program that runs a Factorio-like tick (real inventory rules, inserters, electric networks in brownout, burners) under four architectures with identical work and a checked identical final state. A, static: one class per type, the engine today. B, composed: one class, parts as pointers or bits fixed at construction and checked per tick. C, the runtime ECS the 2018 thread asked for: a component list per entity, virtual update per component, siblings found by tag. D, the template variant: Composed<Input, Energy, Timer, Fluid>, one instantiation per part shape, absent parts as empty bases, their checks removed by if constexpr, one bucket per ticking shape. On a Ryzen 9800X3D under g++ 13 and clang 18, 10 thousand to 1 million entities: B over A 1.05 to 1.10, widest at 1M where the run is memory-bound, the signature of object size (in the model, B's chest is 80 bytes against A's 16); C over A 2.2 to 2.9 and growing with N, because inserters look into their targets' components every tick; D over A 1.00 plus or minus 0.02 at every N under both compilers, the chest shape at 16 bytes like the model's static chest. So the design keeps the shape out of the object layout. D's cost is named too: each shape is one more slot in the per-type update order on every surface, and more instruction cache, which is why the shape set is four and curated. The model is not Factorio: real entities are much larger and most of a real tick is absent.

**What the engine says.** On the busy box at N = 2000 cells (`bench/RESULTS.notes.md`; per-class figures from `bench/results`), one working electric furnace costs 31 ns per tick in its own column (Furnace 0.0626 ms over 2000) and one working assembler 30 ns; the per-cell totals of 78 and 175 ns include two inserters and two infinity chests and are not the machine. A sleeping-furnace world and a chest world have the same entityUpdate within noise (0.00216 against 0.00219 ms at N = 200): sleeping entities are off the update list, as Wube staff have described ("they do not participate in the update loop", FFF-204 discussion, 2017, p=303569). So an S3 instance is projected at about 30 ns per working tick times D over A, and zero in entityUpdate while asleep or S0; an electric sleeper still pays its drain in the electric network update.

The quiet-box rerun (`bench/RESULTS.md`: 3600 ticks, 5 runs, the best run kept, CPUs 8-11 pinned, load average 1.5 to 3.2) measured the native matrix in ns per instance per tick over the empty world, whole update, at N = 2000 and 20000: wooden chests 2 and 0.5, the empty-world floor; idle electric furnaces at the same floor (entityUpdate 0.003 ms at both sizes, as in the empty world); a working electric furnace cell 47 and 64 ns, a stone furnace cell 55 and 61, an assembler cell 100 and 140, each cell again the machine plus two inserters and two infinity chests. The machines' own columns came out lower than on the busy box (Furnace 0.039 ms and AssemblingMachine 0.037 ms over 2000, about 20 and 19 ns each), so 30 ns per working tick is the conservative end. On mulark's public maps (credited to mulark and the map authors, not redistributed), test-000060.no_primed.400x (400x direct-insertion chains) spends 1.73 ms per tick in AssemblingMachine and 0.74 ms in Inserter of 3.95 ms per update.

Each test entity's Lua emulation next to its native comparison, same layout, N = 2000, in ns per instance per tick on the whole update, composed against native, with the composed entity's scriptUpdate share in brackets: cold box 542 against 16 (scriptUpdate 466 of the 542); timed converter 777 against an assembler cell's 100 (625); signal chest 413 against 93 (301); signal endpoint 81 against 15 (64); fluid chest 83 against 22 (59); fuelled chest 152 against 51 (94); seep derrick 149 against 90 (4); dig site 60 against an electric furnace cell's 47 (1). Where the hidden part is a native entity (reactor, EEI, storage tank, combinator) it costs what the native twin costs in its per-class column (Reactor 0.0516 against 0.0514 ms for 2000 fuelled chests), so the gap in the five non-crafters is the polls that stand in for engine wake-ups: scriptUpdate plus extra Lua GC. The two pY-style crafters, whose scripts run only on events, pay 1 to 4 ns of scriptUpdate and carry their gap in the shell's AssemblingMachine column instead (derrick shell 0.153 ms against 0.045 for its twin, converter shell 0.219 against 0.037 for a gear assembler); these runs do not say which part of the shell causes it. Polls that all land on one tick (timed converter, signal chest) keep the median tick near native but push p95 to 5 to 10 ms. The Lua column is the status quo's ceiling; the native type stays projected at the native comparison times D over A. Caveats: the derrick's twin is a chemical plant on lubricant (1 s cycle against 2 s), and the fuelled chest's twin is the emulation's own hidden reactor with no script.

**Each objection, answered.** "The dynamic nature of every bit of logic every component has to do" (2018): parts are fixed at load, the class is static per shape, nothing is looked up by tag. By-type batching gave "zero difference" (2016): not claimed; D over A 1.00 means no loss, not a gain; the claim is no Lua per tick and no hidden entities. Pluggable energy sources "still don't divorce the behavior from the entity" (Nov 2018): agreed; one class handles the energy source as a crafting machine does. Energy source support "means making a given entity updatable which isn't happening for belts" (Dec 2024): new type only. "Massive drain" (2017), answered by Wube itself with raise_on_crafted in 2.1.12: the same opt-in, plus coalescing. Generic per-entity data makes every entity grow (boskid): fixed-size settings, at most 16 bytes, only on this type. A spoil multiplier "would require adjusting spoil tick every time spoilable item is moved between inventories" (boskid, 2025-08-13): this part is exactly that cost, confined to one declared inventory of one opt-in prototype; he did not permit it. The author assumes the re-key itself is one re-stamp of the stack's spoil tick per stack per transfer, with no search and no inventory scan (for Wube to confirm). The cost he means is the hook: each transfer path would gain a check for a modified inventory.

The objections on record, and the answers. A second forum pass (`research/forum-dropped-features.md`, 53 threads not read before, 29 of them in Won't implement) collected every staff refusal that touches a part of this type. Six of them are the ones this writeup has to meet head on.

- "Make the API less rigid" (t=15257, 2015) and custom control behaviours on any prototype (t=69180, 2019): a generic mix-and-match entity "simply will never happen", would mean "throwing out the entirety of the code around entities", would perform so badly "you probably couldn't ever leave red science", and would be un-maintainable. Both describe a runtime-generic system. This type is one static class per shape, reduced at data load, with no code path that asks an entity what it is; the model's variant C is the thing refused, and it measures 2.2 to 2.9 times static, while D measures 1.00. Klonan's pointer in the 2015 thread, Kerbal Space Program's part modules, is the right picture: modules declared in the part's config, fixed when the part loads.
- "Firing events has a performance impact even if zero mods are listening" (t=88647, 2020). True of a global event. The events here are per-prototype opt-in ids, the design Wube shipped as raise_on_crafted in 2.1.12; the cost when no prototype opts in is assumed to be a flag check per finished craft, and nothing at all on prototypes that do not declare the flag (for Wube to confirm).
- "Entities that don't consume power constantly ... are asleep to save CPU time. To make them consume constantly would mean they can't sleep and that's not going to happen" (t=61277, 2018, about a continuous-burn option on vanilla burners). Agreed for vanilla types, which this type does not touch. A composed machine with a burner and activity = "inventory_not_empty" sleeps when empty and burns when holding something; one with activity = "always" is a watcher by the modder's explicit choice on one prototype, costs what a boiler costs, and is reported as such by the loader. The alternative modders use today, a hidden reactor under every machine plus a tick loop (t=108882), is that cost plus Lua.
- Beacons with other energy kinds (t=113632, 2024; t=132603, 2026): refused because a beacon's energy changes are expensive and beacons do not update every tick. The broadcast part is v2 and is listed as debated for exactly this reason; nothing in v1 touches beacons.
- A spoil multiplier on containers (t=134525, 2026-06-28, boskid: "Design decisions around spoilable items make features like this not possible"), after p=678356 (2025). This is the one v1 part Wube has refused twice. The author assumes (`SPEC.md` section 1.3; for Wube to confirm) that the re-key is one re-stamp of a stack's spoil tick, and that the cost is the hook on each transfer path into and out of one declared inventory. It is kept in v1 because the fridge is the most-asked building in the sweep, and it is isolated: cut it and no other part, shape or rule changes.
- Logistic mode on a proxy container (t=136024, 2026, boskid: a proxy "does not own the inventory it borrows, which means it is not instantly notified of content changes"). This one argues for the design: a composed machine owns every inventory it declares, so the transfer notification that wakes inserters and dirties inventory_changed is its own.

Two refusals were reversed later, which is the pattern this writeup counts on: an energy source on offshore pumps ("no interest in making or even supporting", 2020, t=81770) shipped in 2.0, and the runtime per-entity effect that hidden beacons fake shipped as `LuaEntity::local_effect` in 2.1.13 (t=132032), which already retires pY's 2,176 TURD beacons without this type. Of the 29 Won't-implement threads read, 12 give performance as the reason, 11 "it doesn't work that way", 3 maintenance; the design answers the first group with the shape rule and the third with a maintenance surface that is a subset of the crafting machine's.

## 4. Worked examples

**Ice box** (corrundum; today a container plus a 53-tick loop over every box on every surface). Shape S1: a 16-slot storage inventory with spoil multiplier 8 while powered; a burner source on a dry-ice fuel category at 20 kW; activity inventory_not_empty, so dry ice burns only while something is inside. No control stage at all. Items entering while the dry ice burns get eight times their remaining spoil time; leaving, an eighth; running out of fuel re-stamps the 16 slots once and shows no_fuel. Honest differences: corrundum pauses spoilage below 50 percent and rewinds it above, draws dry ice per stack, and exempts Aquilo and Frozeta; the native part slows spoilage by a constant factor. Pause stays reserved: the public API exposes a stack's spoil state as an absolute tick (`LuaItemStack.spoil_tick`), and the author assumes there is no not-started state to return a stack to once it is set (for Wube to confirm), so pause would be a new engine state or a periodic re-stamp that drifts; multiply maps onto the spoil tick as it is.

**Dino dig site** (pY; today an assembler shell permanently disabled by script, a hidden food container, two entities per trip, a 61-tick batch, and a mod-drawn pane for the food slots). Shape S3: a burner source on a dino-food fuel category, so the food is fuel and gets the fuel bar, the no_fuel status, the inserter top-up rule, the ghost request and the read_fuel wire option for free; four module slots restricted to the digosaurus category, so the creatures stay module items; inventories ore (output) and trash; a 20-second automatic cycle, the trip; conditions reporting no_modules and full_output; circuit_outputs contents; event cycle_finished. The handler reads burner.currently_burning for the food's value, picks a scanned ore as the mod does today, inserts the yield, depletes the ore, and sets blocked_status to no_minable_resources when the scan is empty. Deleted: the hidden container, the shell, the loop, the error sprites, the pane, the per-trip entities. One Lua call per trip per site instead of a pass every 61 ticks. Honest note: since 2.1.12 a burner assembler running a real 20-second token recipe with raise_on_crafted could carry the food as fuel and raise the finish event; the creature slots, the yield rule, the status and the "no creature" condition would still be faked around it, and the recipe's product slot would hold a token the player can see.

**Timed converter** (the 2024 request for "a non-machine with input, output, module and fuel inventories", p=687544, unanswered for two and a half years). Shape S3: a filtered input, an output, a burner source, two module slots, a 5-second cycle with start script and consume_at script; events inventory_changed on input and cycle_finished. The first handler calls start_cycle when the mod's rule says the input is convertible; the second removes the stack it chose, inserts the result, and starts again or does not. The request's three complaints are each one field: a tick between crafts, the consumed stack with its tags, the no-craft case.

**Seep derrick** (pY; today a mining drill plus a hidden assembler for the second fluid input, a render object per run, a 67-tick loop). Shape S3 with two fluid boxes: a 2-second cycle with ingredients 10 drilling fluid and products 100 crude oil, blocking when the output is full; a fluid_at_least condition; one uint32 setting named depletion; event cycle_finished. The handler adds the cycle count to the setting, which survives blueprint and undo without a storage table, and inserts the depletion bonus. Deleted: the hidden assembler, the loop, the render object, the fluid API calls.

## 5. Implementation estimate

The build plan is a copy-and-cut of the crafting machine and the container with the recipe half removed, so the maintenance surface is a strict subset of the crafting machine's, minus the part that changes most. `SPEC.md` and the design attempts (not included in the public repository) carry the per-unit line counts; the sum is about 5 to 7 thousand lines of C++ plus docs: prototype and validation 600 to 900, the four shape classes 1500 to 2200, control behaviour 300 to 500, events 250 to 400, Lua surface 400 to 600, GUI and tooltip 1200 to 2000, the rest under 1000. That is comparable to one Space Age entity type, two to four months for one engine developer, the GUI being the long pole. The 3.4 thousand line floor of the leanest draft is what remains if engine-side ingredients and products are cut from v1; offered, not recommended, since the derrick and the Cerys wrecks then run Lua every cycle.

Risks in order: the GUI; inserter rules across several input and storage inventories, where the furnace's special cases live; ghost item requests on a non-crafter (whether the generic item-request-proxy path covers a non-crafter is for Wube to confirm); the spoil re-stamp hooks, one on every transfer path into or out of the declared inventory; tick_interval, whose scheduling and upper bound are assumed from the engine's existing less-than-every-tick updates and are for Wube to confirm; the exact flush point; and the maintenance commitment Rseding91 named in 2020.

## 6. What the Lua reference mod demonstrates, and what it cannot

component-lab (`mod/component-lab/`; its state is in `STATUS.md` there) builds the eight test entities of `docs/ENTITIES.md` with the nearest native shells and hidden parts, so the benchmark measures the status quo exactly and brackets the native type from below. It demonstrates faithfully: every S0 shape (a container with filters and a circuit connector is exact); the process with cycle_finished, because an assembler shell with a fixed_recipe and raise_on_crafted is the real crafting-machine timer with native progress, animation, power draw and status; engine-side ingredients and products, as the shell's real recipe; start = "script" through disabled_by_script, one tick late; modules; script output through a hidden constant combinator; a continuous burner through a hidden reactor. It exhibits the blueprint, undo, cut-paste, clone and mining failure modes, with the handlers' cost measured separately.

It cannot demonstrate: a watcher at near-zero cost per tick (no power-state event, so the cold box polls); coalesced, ordered, engine-raised inventory, status and fluid events (all polls); sleeping at crafter cost with wake on transfer (nothing tells Lua when to re-enable a disabled entity); several inserter-reachable inventories without a proxy container per inventory; per-transfer spoil stamping with a correct unstamp on every exit path (no event fires for an inserter hand or a cursor, so the scan over-counts the native part and gets the semantics wrong); blocked_status and status-driven visuals without render objects; tick_interval; any whole-entity lifecycle without Lua. For each, the benchmark reports the Lua column as the ceiling and marks the native number "not reproducible in Lua", projected from the native comparison times D over A.

Two readings came out of these runs that the earlier ones had missed. A handler on the crafted event runs inside the entity update, so its Lua is billed to the AssemblingMachine column and not to scriptUpdate: the creep collector's harvest is 355 ns per instance there while scriptUpdate reads 7, and every 120 ticks all 2000 shells finish together in a 78 ms tick. The composed type has every one of these by construction, because the parts are its own.

## 7. Beyond v1: the parts that would extend the game

v1 converts 22 of the 189 scripted buildings in the sweep fully and 36 partly. The rest is a map of what was left out, and most of it is cores (inserter, roboport, train stop, silo, unit, drill: 74 rows), recipe selection (17) and cross-entity links (12), none of which this type claims. What is left is parts, and `docs/V2-PARTS.md` ranks them by the rows each one lifts on its own, after a hostile review by a "Wube engineer" persona (not included in the public repository) removed everything that was vanilla already or had no engine mechanism behind it. Every kept part obeys the four v1 rules: fixed at load, off the list when idle, no Lua in the update, wrapping something that exists.

In the same release, a few hundred lines each, none copying another type's update: triggers on cycle start and finish (the projectile, smoke and area-effect machinery weapons use, with the entity as source; script effects forbidden there because a script trigger effect is assumed to raise its Lua in place, for Wube to confirm), which gives Renai's item cannon and SE's delivery cannon with no Lua; typed settings (item, fluid, signal, quality, and an entity-relative offset) that become blueprint parameters the way the constant combinator's signals do today, retiring the prototype-per-value pattern (RealisticReactors keeps 250 reactor prototypes, t=113731); a wildcard ingredient for sinks (three mods generate a recipe per fluid); and small fields: emissions per cycle, the light, destroy-on-empty, fluids in the contents read, editable sections.

In a second step, 1.1 to 1.7 thousand lines, the parts that lift the most rows: electric generation as the electric energy interface's production side filled per cycle or, with a burner, fluid or heat source, in the form of the burner generator, which already pairs a burner with an electric output (one electric source per entity is assumed, so electric in and electric out stays out: 16 sole rows, 12 of them mods, the pY solar family first); heat output as the reactor's heat buffer, filled by the owner in its own update, with conduction left to the heat-pipe pass, assumed to charge the owner nothing (7 rows, five of them Cerys); a circuit enable condition through the enable/disable circuit condition every pump and inserter has, and signal-bound settings pulled by the entity at cycle start, never written from the control-behaviour pass, which is assumed to run separately from the entity update (5 rows); and products from the tile or the surface property table plus placement on resources (5 rows). With those, FULL is at least 64, or 68 counting the four two-blocker rows both of whose blockers ship.

Debated, for a third step: a logistic role per inventory, which is the logistic-member behaviour ten types, eight of them not chests, already have and the thing boskid's refusal of logistic proxy containers argues for (an owned inventory is notified of its own changes, t=136024); and effect broadcast, which would change the beacon's receiver linking, code the effect receiver types share (assumed), and which `LuaEntity::local_effect` (2.1.13) has already made unnecessary for the per-machine cases.

Dropped: a solar-curve generation form (solar priority is SolarPanelPrototype-only and the vanilla panel with solar_coefficient_property already is a tide mill), a continuous process mode (the vanilla boiler takes the full energy-source union and converts through output_fluid_box), two heat buffers or two electric sources on one entity, a status event on passive shapes (a container cannot be frozen and has no update), a surface-typed setting, and a runtime resource condition nothing re-evaluates.

What that buys players, as buildings no mod can ship today (`V2-PARTS.md` section 3, each one prototype and at most one handler): with v1 alone, an aging cellar and a smokehouse (per-container spoil rates, the part boskid refused for containers in 2026), a hearth that burns whether or not it crafts (t=61277 and t=108882: today a hidden reactor and a tick loop), a greenhouse gated by daylight and fed by heat, a radio mast, and a powered-state alarm block (asked since 2014). With the second step, a slag generator (power from a fluid with a solid byproduct, t=120072), a blast furnace that heats a pipe network while it works (t=130444), a reactor whose output is a signal-bound setting, a kiln that burns anything and belches per cycle, a mass driver, a solar still whose output depends on the planet, a hot-rock vent placed on a resource without a drill, and a furnace tuned by circuit. What a Wube engineer would ask for before any of it is in `V2-PARTS.md` section 5: confirmation of how six named mechanisms behave, the shape model re-measured with the energy-out axis, a thread-safety statement for the manager passes, GUI counts, migration rules, and measured numbers for the two parts with a cost story.

The one of those asks already answered is the shape model. `model/RESULTS-v2.md` adds the ticking v2 parts to the workload (an electric or heat output pushed on cycle finish, a circuit enable that sleeps the machine, a trigger on finish) and measures three layouts against hand-written static classes with shuffled update lists: the four v1 shapes with the energy output behind one pointer tested only at the push, 0.95 to 1.02; six machine shapes with one bucket each, 0.96 to 1.01; runtime slots in a class shared with passive entities, 1.08 to 1.12 at a million entities, where a 112-byte chest costs cache lines. Multiplying shapes buys nothing measurable, so v2 keeps four shapes and a null check confined to the push step. The trap the run found is worth stating to Wube: a template that gives each shape its own copy of the accessors inserters call makes those calls polymorphic and cost 12 to 35 percent at ten to a hundred thousand entities; the accessors belong in a core class every shape shares.
